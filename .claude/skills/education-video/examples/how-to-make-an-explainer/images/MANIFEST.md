# Image manifest

GATE 4 of the `education-video` skill, run after the combined audio track was approved and before
Claude Design is briefed. Every row here has to carry a licence that genuinely permits the use, or the
film stops at this gate — which is cheap, unlike stopping after publication.

## What the cue sheet actually needs

All 55 cues were read against one question: is there something here that must be *shown*, as opposed to
drawn? Type, diagrams, charts, waveforms and motion are the picture tool's job.

| scene | what is on screen | photograph needed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Where you're standing | a finished thing, then a person who has to explain it | **no** — a real photograph would name a field, and the film has to travel to any subject |
| 2 Step one | the Claude app or VS Code being opened, a repository installing | **yes, one slot** — this is the only instruction the viewer must follow, and a real screen is more convincing than a drawing of one |
| 3 Three things it has to do | three claims as type, one with a number lit | no |
| 4 Four stages, who does what | the four-stage table, then a split of labour | no |
| 5 Stage one and two | a document with a fact highlighted, a script with slots | no |
| 6 Stage three | a waveform, captions appearing under it, a picture box stretching | no |
| 7 Stage four | a prompt, a paste, a finished video | no |
| 8 Four stages, where to start | the four reversals and their costs, then the link card | no |

**One slot, then.** Everything else in this film is the film's own artifacts, which is the visual
register decided at stage 0: a film that tells you to write a document and then shows one.

## Decided: no photographs at all

**Scene 2 is drawn like the other seven, and nothing of the user's own goes in.** Asked and answered on
2026-08-13, and the reason is the film's reach: material from one particular project makes the film look
like it is about that project, when it has to work for whatever the viewer built. So Claude Design draws
a stylised app window and terminal for the install beat.

**This manifest therefore closes empty, on purpose.** The determination for all 55 cues is in the table
above; that is the gate being run, not skipped. What follows is the reasoning that was weighed for the
one slot that could have carried a real image.

## The one slot that was considered

**Scene 2 could have used a screen capture of the Claude app, or of VS Code with Claude in it, on a real
machine.** It was not filled by me, for reasons that stand whoever fills it:

- **A product UI screenshot needs a licence I cannot establish**, and the honest sources for one are
  the owner's own press kit or the user's own screen. Scraping a frame from someone else's video or a
  marketing page is exactly what this gate exists to prevent.
- **It dates the film.** A real UI changes; the capture would need replacing before the narration does.
- **The user's own screen is the best version of this image anyway** — it is their file, cleanly
  licensed, and it shows the version they are actually telling people to install.

So it was asked for rather than invented, and the answer was to draw it.

## Rules this manifest is held to

- **No watermark is ever removed, cropped out or painted over.** A watermark is the ownership mark on
  an unlicensed preview; taking it off hides that the image is not usable rather than making it usable.
  A watermark is the signal to find another source, said out loud.
- **Every image keeps its untouched original** beside whatever was cropped or cleaned.
- **Delivery is 1920×1080**, so anything soft at full size is discarded rather than upscaled.
