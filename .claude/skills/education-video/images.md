# The image gate

Read this in full before gathering anything. It is GATE 4 of `SKILL.md`, and it runs after the
audio is locked and before the picture is built.

## Open it yourself

**The moment the narration master is locked, say so unprompted:** the picture cannot be briefed
until the images exist, and you are going to go and find candidates now. Do not ask permission to
start. Do not ask the user to supply images first — turning up empty-handed is exactly what makes
this stage get skipped, and a film built without it gets filled with whatever the composition
invents.

## The order, and it is this way round on purpose

1. **Read the cue sheet and list which cues actually need a photograph.** Most do not. Type,
   diagrams, charts and motion are the composition's job. An image is for the thing that must be
   *shown* — a real object, a real place, a real instrument, a real person's work.
2. **Gather candidates yourself, several per slot**, so there is something to choose between.
   Note for each one what it is, where it came from, and its licence, as you go — not afterwards.
3. **Clean them up.** Crop to the film's aspect ratio; remove the background where the subject has
   to sit on the film's own ground; match the delivery resolution; discard anything soft at full
   size. Keep the untouched original beside the edit.
4. **Then show the user the whole set** — each image, what it is, where it is from, its licence,
   and what you changed.
5. **Then, and only then, ask what they want to add of their own**, in a question window. Their
   own photographs of their own work are usually the best images in the film, and they will not
   think to offer them unless asked.

## Licensing is part of the gate, not paperwork afterwards

Every image ships with a source and a licence that genuinely permits the use: public domain, CC0,
CC-BY with the attribution recorded, an explicit licence grant, or the user's own file.

**Never remove, crop out or paint over a watermark.** A watermark is the ownership mark on an
unlicensed preview. Taking it off does not make the image usable — it only hides that it is not.
If the only copy you can find is watermarked, that is the signal to find a different source, and
you say so out loud rather than quietly solving it. The same goes for scraping a frame out of
someone else's video, or a figure out of a paper, without checking what its licence allows.

A film that has to be taken down over an image is worse than a film with one fewer image.

## The manifest

Record the set in the film's own image manifest — one row per image: file, what it shows, which
cue it serves, source URL, licence, attribution string, and what was edited. The composition draws from this manifest,
so an image with no licence stops the film here, which is cheap, rather
than after publication, which is not.

If the film genuinely needs no photographs, say that and record the empty manifest with the
reason. "None needed, everything is drawn" is a legitimate outcome of this gate; skipping the gate
is not.
