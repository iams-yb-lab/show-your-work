# Questions worth asking early

Part of `SKILL.md`. These are the decisions that, answered late, cost a rewrite or a re-record.
**Read the project first** — a brief, a handoff, an earlier film or the user's own message usually
answers most of them. Ask the open ones together, in chat or in a question window, whichever suits
the user; no minimum count, no required form. Offer a concrete recommendation with each.

1. **Audience** — who watches, and what they already know.
2. **Source document** — which file, and whether it is frozen.
3. **Evidence status** — what may be spoken as measured, versus simulated, specified or estimated.
4. **Runtime and structure** — one film or several chapters; minutes and scenes per chapter. This
   sets the word budget.
5. **Scope** — what the film does not cover, in the user's words.
6. **Style guide** — which one applies (the project's, if it has one), or the register wanted.
7. **Vocabulary** — terms to define at first use; terms to avoid; established terminology is used
   as the field uses it.
8. **Voice and languages** — whose voice, which language(s). Each narration language is its own
   master and its own timed picture.
9. **Picture** — any diagram or figure the source document already implies; photographs allowed?
10. **Delivery** — where published, aspect ratio, frame rate, subtitle languages, deadline.
11. **Where the files live** — the directory for documents, scripts and manifests, and the one for
    heavy audio takes and masters. Both belong to the user's project, never beside this skill.

Write the answers down as a short brief in the film's directory, one line per decision, in the
user's words where they used their own. Anything that surfaces later is asked when it surfaces.
