# Voiceover script — How to make an explainer video

Source: [`../SOURCE.md`](../SOURCE.md). Every line ends with the section it came from, after `→`.
Nothing here says anything that document does not, and no number appears that is not a rounding of a
row in [`../NUMBERS.md`](../NUMBERS.md).

**There are deliberately no timecodes.** Nothing has been recorded yet, so every start time and slot
length would be a guess — and the picture would then be cut to the guess. Times get measured off the
frozen recording at stage 3. This file carries words, order and grouping only.

**`[X]` marks the start of a continuous performance** — recorded in one pass, never split mid
sentence. None crosses a scene break.

---

## Scene 1 — Where you're standing

1. [A] You finished the thing. Now other people have to understand it, and none of them were there. → §Where the viewer is standing
2. You want a few clean minutes that explain it, not a course. → §Where the viewer is standing
3. [B] And you don't want to become a video producer to get one. → §Where the viewer is standing
4. You want to hand the work to something that can do it quickly, and still end up with something you'd put your name on. → §Where the viewer is standing
5. That's a reasonable thing to want. It's also, now, a reasonable thing to get — as long as you know which parts are yours to decide. → §Where the viewer is standing

## Scene 2 — Step one

6. [C] Open the Claude app, or VS Code with Claude in it. Ask it to install the repository linked below this video. → §Step one
7. That's the setup. Nothing to configure, no editing software to learn, nothing else to download. → §Step one
8. [D] From there you talk in plain language, and Claude runs four stages. → §Step one
9. It stops at every one to show you what it made and ask whether it's right. → §Step one

## Scene 3 — Three things it has to do

10. [E] Before any of that, three things your video has to do. → §What a good explainer has to do
11. One. The people you aimed it at understand it — the ones you named before writing a word. Aim at everyone, land on nobody. → goal 1
12. Two. Everything in it is true, and you can say why. If something isn't built or measured yet, the video says so. → goal 2
13. A confident voice over a wrong number does more damage than saying nothing. → goal 2
14. [F] Three. Nothing sounds wrong: no clipped word, no hurried sentence, no caption running behind. → goal 3
15. Your viewer won't know why they stopped trusting you. Only that they did. → goal 3

## Scene 4 — Four stages, and who does what

16. [G] Four stages, one order, each finished before the next begins. → §The architecture
17. The document: everything true about your subject, written down and checked. → §The architecture, row 1
18. The script: the words, in order, each with the seconds it needs. → §The architecture, row 2
19. The sound: one recording, frozen — from here on, it owns the clock. → §The architecture, row 3
20. The picture: frames cut to fill lengths already decided. → §The architecture, row 4
21. [H] Claude does all four. The drafting, the counting, the recording, the checking. → §Who does what
22. You do the three things it can't: say who this is for, say what's actually true about your work, and say yes or no at each stage. → §Who does what
23. That last one isn't a formality. A model can't know a number has gone stale. → §Who does what

## Scene 5 — Stage one, and stage two

24. [I] Stage one, the document. Claude asks what the thing is, and writes it down with you. → §Stage 1
25. It presses on which of your numbers are measured and which are only planned, and won't let the same fact in twice. → §Stage 1
26. Your part: tell it the truth about your own work, and say when the document is right. → §Stage 1
27. Because nothing later can repair a wrong number. Animation only enlarges it. → §Stage 1
28. [J] Stage two, the script. Claude turns the document into the words, in order, each line given the seconds it needs so nothing has to be hurried. → §Stage 2
29. It refuses any claim the document doesn't make. → §Stage 2
30. Your part: read it once. It's the last cheap moment to change a word — after this, a change costs a re-recording. → §Stage 2

## Scene 6 — Stage three, the sound

31. [K] Stage three. Claude records it, and freezes one file. → §Stage 3
32. That happens before a single frame exists — and it's the one idea here worth keeping. → §Stage 3, chain
33. A finished picture has a fixed length. Change what's on screen halfway through and you render it again. → §Stage 3, chain 1
34. Speech has no such give. A sentence takes as long as it takes, and read faster, your listener hears the hurry instead of the sentence. → §Stage 3, chain 2
35. [L] So whichever one you finish first becomes the box the other has to fit. → §Stage 3, chain 3
36. Finish the picture first and the sentence is what gets damaged: cut short, hurried, or spliced — and all three are audible. → §Stage 3, chain 4
37. Finish the sound first and the picture absorbs it instead. A diagram resting a moment longer is invisible. → §Stage 3, chain 5
38. So the thing that can't be squeezed sets the clock. → §Stage 3, chain 6
39. [M] From that one file, the captions come off word by word — and so does every length the picture gets cut to. → §Stage 3
40. Claude also checks the recording actually said the script. On one film, five lines were rewritten because a word had gone missing. → §Stage 3, `TRIAL.reworded`
41. [N] Your part: listen. Timing and pronunciation can be checked automatically. Whether it sounds like a person cannot. → §Stage 3
42. Two complete soundtracks were thrown out here after passing every check there was. That's why the file comes to you. → §Stage 3, `VOICE.binned`

## Scene 7 — Stage four, one paste

43. [O] Stage four, the picture. Every length is already decided, so it has one job: fill the time it's given. → §Stage 4
44. And from where you sit, it's one step. Claude hands you a single prompt. → §Stage 4, the paste
45. It already carries the scene list, what each scene shows, what the video should look like, and where every file is sitting on your disk. → §Stage 4, the paste
46. [P] You paste it into Claude Design, and wait. → §Stage 4, the paste
47. What comes back is a finished video with the sound already in it. Not a silent picture you then have to combine with something. → §Stage 4, the paste

## Scene 8 — Four stages, and where to start

48. [Q] Document, script, sound, picture. Do it backwards and each step has its own price. → §The verdict
49. Picture first is the normal way, and the expensive one: the sentence is what gets damaged. → §The verdict, alternative 1
50. Document last, and the video becomes where your thinking happens — errors at full volume, in a confident voice. → §The verdict, alternative 2
51. Skip the script, and a problem you'd have fixed with a keystroke costs a whole re-record. → §The verdict, alternative 3
52. Sound last, and nothing sets the clock, so every length is a guess. → §The verdict, alternative 4
53. [R] All four stages come out of one repository. The link is below this video. → §The skill
54. Built and verified with Claude. Other models haven't been tested — that's unknown, not broken. → §The skill
55. Now go and explain the thing you made. → §The verdict
